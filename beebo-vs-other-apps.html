AN HONEST, ANONYMOUS COMPARISON

Choose the service that fits your home.

Beebo is newer than three established personal-media services. This page explains what each type of service is good at, where Beebo is different, and what Beebo is still building — organized by the platform or device you actually watch on.

Beebo release status, plainly: A pale-green tick means that feature is already built in Beebo's current release candidate. Testing verifies it on real devices; it does not mean the feature still needs to be built before we call it fully released. A gold mark means the feature is still being built or is in beta. We do not label either state as fully released until the final checks pass.
Why no company names? This is designed to help you compare approaches, not to borrow another company's reputation. Features, devices and prices change often, so always check the provider you are considering before purchasing. This page was reviewed September 23, 2026.

Where Beebo is focused

  • A friendly Windows-first home library for media you already own.
  • Household setup, phone connection, trips, campsite tools and shared games in one experience.
  • Clear labels for features that are ready, in beta or still in development.
  • Simple local setup first, with away-from-home options designed to be understandable.

Where established services are ahead

  • More years of real-world testing and larger support communities.
  • More mature apps across television, Apple, Linux, NAS and other platforms.
  • Broader plug-in ecosystems and integrations.
  • More established live-TV, DVR, music and home-theater support.

What costs money — and what does not

Beebo at home: Your personal library, playback on your own home network, household profiles, and local Campsite activities are free. Beebo self-hosted away from home: if you use your own port forwarding, UPnP, relay, or VPN, Beebo does not charge a per-stream fee or impose a Beebo household stream limit. Your computer, upload speed, electricity, and any service you choose remain your responsibility.

Beebo-managed away-from-home: In development — expected soon, pending final security and real-device testing. The planned CA$3 household option includes up to 2 simultaneous managed remote streams. The planned CA$5 option includes up to 4. These limits apply only while Beebo supplies the managed connection. Beebo VPN: In development — expected soon, pending final security and real-device testing. Planned at CA$2 per household, with up to 4 managed VPN connections.

Other services: "available" does not always mean "free." A feature can require a subscription, one-time mobile unlock, supported hardware, a tuner, app-store purchase, or the customer's own server. The table above calls out those differences.

Beebo's advanced adaptive playback and video conversion

Built and awaiting broader device testing: Beebo is designed to make a playback decision for the device watching now. It prefers original-file direct play, then compatible stream copy/remuxing, and only starts an HLS conversion when a device, subtitle, audio format, selected quality, or connection requires it.

  • Original quality first: compatible devices receive the original video without re-encoding.
  • Targeted processing: a compatible video can be repackaged without converting its picture; Beebo changes only the part that needs work where possible.
  • Hardware-aware conversion: the engine tests usable NVIDIA, Intel Quick Sync, AMD, Apple and VAAPI encoder paths, then can fall back to software conversion.
  • Stable home computer: conversion and remux work are limited so one demanding stream does not consume every available slot.
  • Picture and sound decisions: device capability, resolution, HDR tone-mapping path, audio compatibility, subtitles and requested quality are part of the plan.
  • Clear reason: the playback plan records why Beebo chose direct play, a compatible stream, or conversion.

These are real implementation goals and code paths. Real-world performance still varies by source file, device, graphics hardware, drivers and network. Read the complete technical explanation.

Technical comparison guide

Compare the numbers, the conditions, and the price

Streaming products often use the same words for different things. This guide separates original-file playback, stream repackaging, and conversion. It also shows whether a capability is included, paid, or an optional upgrade.

$ IncludedUsable without paying that company for the feature. $ Paid serviceRequires that company's paid plan, unlock, or hosted service. $ Optional upgradeAvailable without it; the paid option adds the stated benefit.

Direct Play

What it is: the original file travels to a device that already supports its container, video, audio, and subtitle tracks. No conversion is needed. Why it matters: original quality with very low server load.

DirectStream / remux

What it is: compatible video is repackaged for the player. Beebo uses fragmented MP4/CMAF HLS for this route. Why it matters: avoids re-encoding compatible video, but it is not identical to Direct Play.

On-demand conversion

What it is: the server makes a compatible stream while it plays. Beebo's H.264 HLS segments are 4 seconds; it creates segments near the viewing point and removes old session segments. Why it matters: compatibility for a device or connection that cannot play the original.

Capacity needs a condition

A simultaneous-conversion number must state the computer and quality involved. Beebo's protective starting policy is 1 conversion on a weak PC, 2 on a typical PC, and one more with a working graphics encoder; the owner can adjust it after testing.
