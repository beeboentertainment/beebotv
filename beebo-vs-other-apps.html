Beebo vs Plex, Jellyfin and Emby

Plex, Jellyfin and Emby are established, widely used personal media servers. Beebo is newer and smaller. Here is a fair side-by-side, with a status label on every Beebo feature so you can see what you can use today and what is still coming.

Checked September 21, 2026. As of September 2026 and only for the versions named on this page: Beebo for Windows 0.1.58 and the Beebo Android app 1.40.

The short version. Plex, Jellyfin and Emby are ahead of Beebo on device choice, server choice, add-ons and years of polish. Beebo’s case is narrower: the app is free, your library and video stay on your own PC, away-from-home viewing is free with a direct connection or your own relay, and it has things we did not find in the other three, such as Campsite Mode. If you need a NAS server, Roku or Apple TV today, one of the three may suit you better. If you run Windows and watch on Android and in a browser, Beebo may suit you well. For a living-room setup, see Home theater and big screen.

What Beebo adds

These are the things we built Beebo around. We did not find them on the official pages of the other three, checked September 21, 2026. If we missed one, please tell us and we will correct this page.

Your library stays on your PC Available now

The app is free to use at home. Your library and video don’t go to Beebo: Beebo’s servers handle sign-in and introductions, and video goes straight between your devices, or through a relay if you choose one. Some optional features talk to other services, and each is listed in Privacy in plain words.

Away from home, built in Available now

Every Beebo gets a free address like yourname.beebo.tv, plus a connection test and a guided choice of direct connection, automatic UPnP, your own relay, or the optional paid Beebo Relay. See Watch away from home.

Campsite Mode Available now

On Android, your phone makes a local Wi-Fi so guests can watch the videos you downloaded, or play dozens of shared party games, in their own browser with no app and no account. Playing the same music in sync on many phones, with an optional left and right pair, is Available now — test build (Beta) in the Android 1.40 test build, and phones as movie surround speakers are In development. See Turn every phone into a speaker and the Campsite Mode guide.

Trip Journal Available now — test build (Beta)

Tap “We’re heading out” and “We’re home”, and Beebo gathers the trip’s games, stories, badges and packing list into a recap you can present as a slideshow or save as a video. Android only, and new. A private share link served from your own PC needs Android 1.39 or later and Windows 0.1.58. See the Trip Journal guide.

Beebo Auto and Watch Together Available now

Beebo Auto is an audio-only car companion for your library. Watch Together keeps passenger screens in sync between Beebo apps signed in to the same account. See Beebo on the road.

Works with more apps Available now — Beta (Windows 0.1.58)

An optional mode, off by default, that lets some Jellyfin-compatible apps browse and play your Beebo library. It is experimental: checked against the official Jellyfin API description and SDK, but not tried with real Jellyfin apps. Beebo is not Jellyfin and is not affiliated with that project. See Jellyfin apps with Beebo.

Live TV, audiobooks, skip intro and themes Available now — Beta (Windows 0.1.58)

Live TV and DVR with your own HDHomeRun tuner (never tested on a real tuner), audiobooks from your own files, automatic intro and credits skipping, and theme and layout packs arrived in Windows 0.1.58.

Karaoke-style lyrics Available now — Beta (Windows 0.1.58)

Lyrics from your own .lrc files already show while a song plays. Recording your own voice over a backing track on the web Music page arrived in Windows 0.1.58 (Beta). See Karaoke and recordings.

Where Plex, Jellyfin and Emby are ahead today

  • Devices. All three have apps for Roku, Apple TV, Samsung and LG TVs and more. Beebo runs on Android TV and Google TV and casts to Chromecast, and its other TV apps are in development and not published.
  • Server choice. All three run on Windows, Linux, macOS, Docker and many NAS brands. Beebo’s server is Windows first, with Linux and Docker in Beta.
  • Maturity and community. They have years of development, large communities and many third-party tools. Beebo is a small, newer company.
  • Add-ons. Jellyfin and Emby have plugin ecosystems. Beebo deliberately has none, only a small read-only API in Beta.
  • Live TV and DVR. All three have it now. Beebo’s is Beta and needs your own HDHomeRun tuner.
  • Open source. Jellyfin is open source today. Beebo is not, and an open-source direction is only Planned.
  • No company account. Jellyfin says it has no central servers collecting your data. Beebo uses a small service for sign-in and introductions.

Who should choose what

Beebo

  • You run Windows 10 or 11 at home.
  • You watch on Android, in a browser, or on Android TV and Google TV.
  • You want away-from-home viewing without a pass or a reverse proxy.
  • You want your library and video kept on your own PC.
  • You go camping or on road trips, and want Campsite Mode.
  • You are happy with a newer app that is still growing.

Plex

  • You want the widest choice of devices today.
  • Your server will be a NAS, Mac or Linux box.
  • You are happy to pay for a Plex Pass for the extras.
  • You like Plexamp for music.

Jellyfin

  • You want open source and no paid tier at all.
  • You enjoy running your own reverse proxy or VPN.
  • You want plugins, free Live TV and books support.

Emby

  • You want wide device support and one payment for Premiere.
  • You want DVR, or CarPlay and Android Auto.
  • You are happy with a closed-source product.

Questions

Is Beebo really free?

The Beebo app is free to use at home. Watching away from home is also free with a direct connection, automatic UPnP or your own relay. Only optional Beebo services, such as Beebo Relay, are paid. See the pricing section for current details.

What do the labels mean, and what is in the current download?

Available now means it is in Beebo for Windows 0.1.58 and the Android app 1.40. Available now — Beta means it is in the current download but is new and still being tested on real equipment. Available now — test build (Beta) means it is in the current Android download, a test build that has not yet been checked on real phones. In development and Planned mean it is not usable, with no date. See What’s new for releases.

Does Beebo show ads?

Ads are off by default. If Beebo ever offers optional ad-supported credit, it will only be switched on if you opt in, can be turned off any time, and is never required to use the app.

Does Beebo have a game emulator or a ROM launcher?

No. Beebo does not include an emulator, does not run console games or ROM files, and does not supply games of that kind. If you have read otherwise in a search summary or on an older page, that is wrong.

Does Beebo mirror a phone onto a car screen or get around driving restrictions?

No. Beebo does not offer screen mirroring to a car display. Beebo Auto works inside Google’s Android Auto and follows its rules. Games and video-style features there are for passengers only, never for the driver, and Beebo does not bypass driver-distraction protections.

Can Beebo convert video with my computer’s graphics hardware?

Beebo for Windows 0.1.58 checks which hardware video encoder your PC has (for example Intel Quick Sync) and uses it when it works, and falls back to software conversion when it does not. It is new and has been tested on a small range of hardware, so we call it Beta.

Can more than one person use one Beebo server?

Yes. An owner can add people with their own sign-in, watch history and parental controls, in a household of up to six profiles. See parental controls and the docs.

Which devices does Beebo run on?

Windows, Linux and Docker on the server side, Android phones and tablets, and any web browser. Other TV and Apple apps are In development. The table above and the platforms page show the status of each one.

Is Beebo open source?

Not open source yet. The source code is published for transparency at github.com/SWGfan/beebo-app, but no open-source licence has been granted. An open-source direction is Planned, with no date.

Should I move from Plex, Jellyfin or Emby to Beebo?

Only if Beebo fits how you watch. It is newer and smaller, and its server is best supported on Windows: Linux and Docker are Beta. Beebo does not move or change your files, so you can point it at the same folders and try it beside your current server. Windows 0.1.58 includes an importer (Beta) for watched marks, resume points, ratings and playlists from Plex, Jellyfin, Emby, Kodi and Letterboxd, with a review step and an undo.
