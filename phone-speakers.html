How it works, in four steps

  1. Start Campsite Mode on the host phone

    The host is the Android phone with the Beebo app. Queue some songs in Music, then open Campsite Mode and tap Invite guests. Choose how the other phones will reach yours: Beebo’s own Wi-Fi, your phone’s hotspot, or a Wi-Fi everyone is already on.

  2. Guests join and open Music

    Each guest scans the codes on the host’s screen. Their phone joins the Wi-Fi and opens the Campsite page in an ordinary browser. They open the Music tab. No app, no account.

  3. Each guest taps once to turn sound on

    Phone browsers only allow sound after a tap, so each guest taps Tap to enable audio. Every phone has its own volume control.

  4. The host taps Play together

    Every phone plays the same song at the same moment. A phone that joins late starts partway through. Pause, next and previous on the host apply to everyone. The host can set each phone to Left, Right or All, so two phones become a left and right stereo pair. The group can still choose any connected compatible device as the main screen; a phone can be the main screen or remain a speaker.

How the sync works, in plain words. Each guest phone checks how far its clock is from the host phone’s, many times, in the style of internet time servers. It then schedules the song to start at an agreed moment using the browser’s audio clock, and keeps checking. If a phone drifts, it speeds up or slows down by a tiny fraction of a percent, which is designed to be too small to hear. Step-by-step help is in the Phone speakers guide.

What you need

  • One Android phone as the host, with the Beebo Android app 1.39 or later, the test build you can download from this site. Earlier versions do not have the feature.
  • Music in your Beebo library. The host phone gets the songs it plays from your Beebo computer, so it must be able to reach that computer to load them. Music that is only stored on a phone is not read.
  • A phone, tablet or laptop with a web browser for each guest. The page is built for current phone browsers, but we have not yet checked it on real phones, and an older browser may not support it. We do not claim it works on every phone or browser.
  • A Wi-Fi the guests can join: Beebo’s own Wi-Fi, the host’s hotspot, or a shared Wi-Fi that lets phones see each other. Some hotel and public Wi-Fi does not.
  • Music you have the right to play. Beebo includes no music, movies or shows.

What to expect, honestly

Sync accuracy

  • Tested on one computer with two browser windows, and in simulation. Not yet measured on real phones over a hotspot.
  • We expect differences of tens of milliseconds. That suits group listening. It is not a calibrated stereo system, so treat the left and right pair as a fun party feature.
  • Each phone shows a Sync indicator: green “In sync” when its estimated error is within about 30 ms. It is an estimate, and it cannot see delay added by a speaker.

Speakers and delay

  • Bluetooth speakers add delay, often a lot more than a phone’s own speaker.
  • Each guest page has a −10 ms / +10 ms trim to line a phone up by ear.
  • Each joined phone displays its assigned position before playback starts. The current layouts can label phones as Left, Right or All; movie layouts can use available positions such as Front left, Front right, Centre, Back left, Back right and Subwoofer.
  • Planned wireless-speaker option. Beebo is designed to work through the connection method a phone supports, such as Bluetooth, Chromecast or a speaker maker’s own wireless system. Brand compatibility and real-device timing still need testing before this is marked available. Wireless-speaker delay varies, so the final feature will include placement and trim controls for a quick group test.

Screens and browsers

  • Keep the page open with the screen on. Some phones pause a web page when the screen locks, and we have not yet tested ways around that.
  • Leave the page and the music stops on that phone. Come back and it catches up by itself.
  • iPhones may ask for the audio tap again after a call or a lock.

The host phone

  • In this first version the host phone is the conductor and stays quiet.
  • The design point is about eight guest phones on one hotspot.
  • Songs longer than 12 minutes are skipped.

Movie and video surround: phones as speakers Available now — Beta (Windows 0.1.58, simulated only)

Everything above is about music. Beebo for Windows 0.1.58 adds a movie and video version. Choose the connected compatible device that makes the best main screen for the group, then place each phone as a speaker. Depending on the selected layout and the film, a phone can be assigned front left, front right, centre, surround, back left, back right or subwoofer. The picture screen’s own sound is muted while phones are playing.

How it works

  • Choose the main screen. On the details page of a film or episode, choose Phone speakers and select the connected compatible device that will show the picture. The person must be signed in and allowed to watch that title, so parental controls still apply.
  • Join by QR code. A window shows a QR code. Put it on the big screen. Guests scan it with their phone, type a name, tap Join, then tap once to enable audio. No app, no account, no internet: it works on your home Wi-Fi.
  • Press play. The picture is the master clock, and the phones follow it. After a seek the picture waits until the phones have caught up.
  • Choose the layout and place the phones. Surround (for 5.1 and 7.1 films), a stereo pair, or every phone playing the whole film folded to one channel. Every phone shows its position, such as Front left or Back right. A seating chart lets you move phones between available positions, mute one, or nudge its timing. If a layout has enough phones, it can use the additional positions available in that layout, including centre, surround and subwoofer.
  • If a phone leaves, the TV can play the missing channel, the nearest phone can cover it, or it can stay silent. With no phone playing, the film’s own sound returns on the screen.

What you need

  • A PC running Beebo for Windows 0.1.58, holding the film.
  • A connected compatible phone, tablet, TV or computer to show the picture.
  • Phones with an ordinary web browser, on the same home Wi-Fi. By default only phones on your home network can join.

What has and has not been tested

  • Measured on one computer: splitting a film’s sound into one channel per phone, using the ffmpeg that ships with Beebo.
  • Simulated: the whole flow was run between two desktop browser windows, one as the TV and one as a phone. Sync figures are from simulation, not from real Wi-Fi.
  • Not tested: real phones, a real TV browser, or a real Wi-Fi network. Small phone speakers cannot play deep bass, so a phone playing the subwoofer channel will not sound like a subwoofer. Bluetooth speakers add delay. It is a party and campsite feature, not a calibrated home-theater system, and it does not replace a soundbar or receiver.
  • At any playback speed other than normal, the phones stay quiet and the film’s own sound returns on the screen.

Privacy: guests are anonymous nicknames and need no account or internet connection. Your film’s sound is cut on your own PC and sent to the phones over your own Wi-Fi.

Not yet Planned

  • Campfire story voices on different phones are planned. There is no date and nothing usable is built.
  • Playing the music on the host phone in sync with the guests is not built.
  • None of the phone-speaker features has been tested on real phones over real Wi-Fi.

Questions about phone speakers

Does Beebo Campsite Mode sync sound across multiple phones?

For music, Beebo Campsite Mode can play the same track in sync on many phones and can split it into a left and right stereo pair. The group chooses a connected compatible device as its main screen, while the other phones remain speakers. Each phone shows its position before playback begins. The Android feature is a Beta test build and has not yet been measured on real phones. Movie and video surround is a separate Windows beta, tested only in simulation.

Can I try it today?

Yes, as a test. It is in the Android test build 1.40 (build 41), which you download and install from this site (see Get Beebo on your phone). It is Beta: it is still being tested and has not been checked on real phones over a hotspot, so expect rough edges. Older Android versions do not have it.

Does it need the internet?

The guests’ phones do not. They join the host phone’s Wi-Fi (Beebo’s own Wi-Fi, the phone’s hotspot or a shared Wi-Fi), and the music travels over that Wi-Fi. The host phone gets its songs from your Beebo computer, so it needs to be able to reach that computer to load them.

Do guests need an app or an account?

No. A guest scans the code and opens the page in an ordinary web browser on a phone, tablet or laptop, then taps once to turn sound on. They install nothing and sign up for nothing. The host needs the Beebo Android app.

How many phones can play together?

We designed it for about eight guest phones on one hotspot. Each guest downloads each song from the host phone, so more phones mean more Wi-Fi traffic and a longer wait before a song starts. We have not tested large groups on real phones, so we do not promise a number.

Will I be able to use a wireless speaker box?

That is planned. Beebo is being designed to use the wireless connection a phone supports, such as Bluetooth, Chromecast or a speaker maker’s own wireless system, including portable job-site and home speakers. Brand compatibility and real-device timing need testing before it is marked available. Wireless audio can add delay that Beebo cannot measure, so the final feature will include speaker placement and a trim control for a quick group test.

Does it work for movies and videos?

Yes, in a beta. Beebo for Windows 0.1.58 can use phones as separate speakers for a movie or video. Choose a connected compatible device for the picture, then assign phones to the available audio positions, such as Front left, Front right, Centre, Back left or Back right. We have tested it in simulation and by measurement on one computer, not on real phones. Campsite Mode can also let guests watch downloaded videos together on their own phones, where each phone plays the whole soundtrack.

Does the host phone play the music too?

Not in this first version. The host phone is the conductor: it stays quiet and starts, pauses and skips for everyone. Playing on the host phone in sync as well is not built.

How accurate is the sync?

In our tests on one computer, two browser windows reported the same point in a song within a fraction of a millisecond, and a simulated phone with a badly drifting clock stayed within about 13 milliseconds. That is not the same as real phones on a hotspot, which we have not measured yet. We expect differences of tens of milliseconds, which suits group listening but is not exact enough to call a calibrated stereo system. Each phone shows a Sync indicator that turns green when its estimated error is within about 30 ms.

Will it keep playing when a phone’s screen locks?

Keep the page open with the screen on. Some phones pause a web page when the screen locks, and we have not tested ways to keep it playing. If a phone drops out, open the page again and it catches up by itself. iPhones may ask for the audio tap again after a call or a lock.
