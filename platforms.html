Where each part runs

The server: Windows, with Linux and Docker in beta

Your movies and shows stay on one computer you own, and that computer has to be on while people watch from it. The main version runs on Windows 10 or 11. A Linux version and a Docker image for NAS boxes and servers are available as betas.

The viewers: Android, any browser, and your TV

Watch with the Android app on a phone or tablet, or in any web browser. For the big screen, cast to a Chromecast or Google TV, or run the Android app on Android TV or Google TV. Apps for Fire TV, Roku, Samsung, LG, Xbox and Apple TV are in development and not available yet. The Home theater and big screen page covers video conversion, TV apps, servers and picture and sound formats. The Playing on TVs guide and the NAS and Docker guide have the steps.

Away from home

Every Beebo gets a free address like yourname.beebo.tv. Open it on mobile data or any Wi-Fi, sign in, and your library is there. Beebo tries a direct connection to your home PC first. See Watch away from home for the options, and how Beebo compares with other media server apps, including where they are ahead. Questions about devices

Which devices does Beebo work on today?

The Beebo server runs on a Windows 10 or 11 PC. You watch on Android phones and tablets with the Android app, in any web browser (including Safari on iPhone and iPad), and on a TV by casting to a Chromecast or Google TV. Android TV and Google TV can also run the Android app directly. A Linux desktop version and a Docker image are available as betas. The Home theater and big screen page has the full status.

Does Beebo work on Windows 10?

Yes. Beebo’s server and app run on Windows 10 and Windows 11. The PC needs to be on while you stream from it, and you supply your own movie and show files.

Is there a Beebo app for iPhone, iPad or Apple TV?

Not yet. Native Apple TV, iPhone and iPad apps are in development, are not published or tested on real devices, need Apple’s approval, and have no date. Today, iPhone and iPad can watch in Safari, and you can send video to an Apple TV with AirPlay from Safari on an iPhone, iPad or Mac. Offline downloads need the Android app.

Is there a Beebo app for Roku, Samsung, LG, Fire TV or Xbox?

Not yet. Apps for Roku, Samsung and LG smart TVs, Amazon Fire TV and Xbox are in development, with no release dates and no tests on real devices yet. In the meantime you can cast from the Android app to a Chromecast or Google TV, or run the Android app on Android TV or Google TV.

Can I run the Beebo server on Linux?

Yes, as a beta. Beebo 0.1.58 has a Linux download as an AppImage and a .deb package, the same desktop server as on Windows. It was built and tested on Ubuntu 22.04 in our automated build only, not on other distributions or desktops, and Linux has no automatic updates yet. A headless Docker image for servers and NAS boxes is also in beta. See the Linux page.

Does Beebo work for a home theater?

Partly, and we say which part. Android TV and Google TV, and casting are available now. Video conversion with your PC’s graphics card, Linux and Docker servers and Live TV are available in Windows 0.1.58 as betas. Apps for Fire TV, Roku, Samsung, LG, Xbox and Apple devices, and passthrough of HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Atmos and DTS:X, are in development, so Beebo does not claim them. See Home theater and big screen.

Does Beebo cost anything?

Beebo Entertainment is free to use at home. Away-from-home access is also free with a direct connection, automatic UPnP, or your own relay, at any resolution. Beebo Relay, Beebo's own relay servers, is an optional paid fallback for households where none of those work: about CA$3/month for 1080p or CA$5/month for 4K, priced by streaming quality rather than data used. No payment card required to get started.
