One company. One set of servers. A few different jobs.

Beebo started as a personal media server. We're building it out into a small family of products that all run on the same infrastructure — so nothing sits idle, and the savings go into keeping prices low instead of into running separate servers for separate products.

Beebo Relay

When a direct connection, automatic UPnP, or your own relay isn't possible, Beebo Relay carries your video the rest of the way through our managed relay. Encryption and connection details depend on the selected route. It's an optional paid fallback, not something you need just to watch away from home.

  • Direct connection, automatic UPnP, and your own relay are all tried first, always — all $0
  • Planned CA$3/month for two simultaneous managed streams or CA$5/month for four; final billing and service limits await verification
  • Runs on the same servers as everything else Beebo does

Why we can keep this affordable

Most companies run a separate set of servers for every product — one for media relay, another for VPN, another for game hosting — and a lot of that capacity sits unused most of the time. Beebo runs all of its products on the same servers instead, with separate quotas so one product can never slow another down. That efficiency is what lets most households pay nothing for away-from-home access in the first place, and lets us price Beebo Relay by streaming quality instead of by the gigabyte when you do need it — it's the model behind everything else we build.

As a region gets enough households actually paying for away-from-home access, we plan to add server capacity closer to them, so connections stay fast without relying on one central location. This isn't automatic or instant — every new region goes through a real cost-and-reliability check and a human decision before it happens, the same way any responsible infrastructure change should. But the intent is straightforward: grow where people actually are, funded by what they're already paying, not by raising prices to cover empty capacity elsewhere.
