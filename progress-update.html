PROGRESS REPORT · OCTOBER 23–24, 2026

What we did. What worked. What still needs work.

Current correction — Oct 26, 2026, 9:20 AM Eastern

The corrected current average is 64% across 70 features; device and real-world checks recorded are 27%. Three rows remain at 99%; none is at 100%. No row went up. Earlier campaign counts below overlap and are historical, not fresh totals. Read the new failures and bounded passes.

Updated Oct 24, 2026, 9:15 PM Eastern.

Across October 23 and October 24, we checked Beebo on real phones, a real TV and real Windows PCs, including a second household on another network. We found working parts. We also found bugs. This is the full picture, including the work that is still switched off.

Our overall checklist score is 70%. Android is 78%. Windows is 72%. Other systems are 57%. Services and the website are 50%. These are weighted checks across 70 feature rows. They are not a percentage of users who will have a trouble-free experience. A 99% row still needs Nick's final human look. No row has that final Ready check.

Today's real-device checks.

Final phone table: 127 passed, 15 failed, 16 blocked.

That is 158 active assessments across 32 Android feature rows. There were 142 passed or failed assessments. A feature has several checks; this does not mean 127 features work.

We carried 110 earlier passes without rerunning them. The fifth round added 17 active passes, four failures and three blocks, replacing older prerequisite records. Withdrawn diagnostic history remains separate. Three extra library-sharing cases are blocked outside the 32-row count.

Installed Android 1.51-tester36, build 86, was exercised on 26 of the 32 rows, including rows that failed. The remaining six need external hardware, distribution or implemented-build prerequisites. Older tester35 passes retain their own date and build. S24 guest evidence is Chrome 155.0.8059.84, not its Beebo app. Later native code is not installed or accepted by these tests.

The final phone report was assembled October 24 at 3:43:02 AM Eastern. Each row keeps its actual Last PASS time; a new failure never refreshes that time. Fifteen cross-device scenarios separately recorded 10 passes, one failure and four blocks. Those overlap the other evidence and must not be added to its totals.

Windows and web: 58 passed, 1 failed, 38 blocked.

We ran server, browser, API and media-output checks on a real Windows PC. The run used the Windows 0.1.99 development runtime and Chrome 155.0.8059.85. It did not launch the installed Windows application.

There were 21 carried passes and 37 new passes. The checks covered conversion, an own-playlist TV feed, Movie Night, compatible media-client APIs, profile rules, synthetic space-saving, volume-levelled media output, browser music controls, picture frame and kitchen announcements. Each is a smaller tested scope.

The final Windows and web record was assembled October 23 at 9:00:14 PM Eastern. Each feature keeps its own actual Last PASS time. A report time does not turn an old pass into a fresh test.

Seventeen browser games had named interactions checked. Seven rounds finished: Tic Tac Toe, Drop Four, Chess, Five Dice, Snakes and Ladders, Dots and Boxes, and Memory Match. This does not clear every mode, every browser, touch controls or real online opponents.

Our test process uses AI testers on real devices in realistic situations. Muted playback is not a listening check. A browser control test is not a native Windows-app test. A missing fixture, account, route or piece of hardware stays blocked.

October 24 afternoon and evening: away from home, a second household, the TV and the browser.

Added Oct 24, 2026, 9:15 PM Eastern. These are real-device checks run by AI helpers, with the households’ owners approving the sign-ins and the relay terms. Most phone checks used an unpublished phone test build (tester40 to tester42), not a published download. No percentage, checkmark or status label changed. Each line says what was tried and what was not.

Away from home: direct, then Beebo Relay.

About 1:30 PM Eastern: a Galaxy S24 on 5G mobile data only (Wi-Fi off) reached the home server directly, with no relay, and played a TV episode. The clock advanced 21 seconds in 20 seconds with no stall, and the route screen read “Direct over the internet, no relay”.

About 3:40 PM: with “always use Beebo Relay” on, the route screen read “Beebo Relay” and a film played over 5G, but the clock advanced only 12 seconds in 20 seconds. That is slower than real time, and nothing longer was measured.

Not tested: Wi-Fi to mobile-data hand-over and sessions over a few minutes.

A real second household, through Beebo Relay.

About 7:50 to 8:05 PM: a different Windows PC at another house (Beebo 0.1.102, which it had updated itself to at 5:41 PM) had its household owner accept the relay terms with the new “Use Beebo Relay” button; its log moved from “relay beebo unavailable” to “relay beebo ready”. The S24 on 5G signed in through that household’s Beebo address, browsed its library, connected through Beebo Relay in about 2 seconds and played the original file of a film with picture and sound (clock 0:51 to 1:32 in 40 seconds).

Failed: choosing a converted 720p quality on that old 4-core PC failed twice (“Playback interrupted”; the conversion session opened and stopped, and the log did not say why).

Not tested: a converted quality that works on that PC, a direct route to that PC, and two people on Relay at once. Casting on mobile data shows an honest “Casting needs Wi-Fi” message.

Chromecast on the real TV (test builds).

About 1:00 to 2:40 PM, with the owner watching the Living Room TV: the on-screen play, pause and skip controls were dimmed and could not be tapped while casting. A TV-volume row added on Oct 20 had grown the top bar over the buttons. The fix was reviewed and merged into the main code line at about 3:45 PM; it is in test builds only. On the real device the owner paused and played by finger, skipped, opened the quality menu and stopped casting.

About 8:20 PM, test build 42: a streamed film cast and played on the TV with picture and sound; pause, play, a 15-second skip forward and stop casting worked, and the TV left cast mode.

Failed: casting a downloaded copy showed “Casting” on the phone but the TV did not play, because a TV cannot reach a file saved on the phone. An honest message is a small open item. Still unproven: the phone pass-through route (a fix for a likely cause is written and passes 16 new tests but is not merged), seeking by dragging, captions on the TV and a second sender.

Captions in the browser player.

About 8:25 PM, browser player on the S24 over 5G through Beebo Relay (home server 0.1.102): a tiny synthetic film with a separate subtitle file listed “English (file)”, and three cues drew at the right times (4.3, 9.7 and 15.0 seconds) and again after seeking back.

This is the browser player. The Android app’s own captions have a fix merged into the code line (in test build 42) that no phone has shown yet. Burned-in captions on converted video and other formats were not tested.

Chrome on a PC: passes and four failures.

Passed with no Beebo console errors: playback, pause, Left and Right arrow (minus and plus 10 seconds), quality change 480p, 720p, 480p, search, By Actor, By Release Date, Music, Audiobooks, Playlists and Watchlist.

Failed (fixes written, not merged): Space does not pause and F does not go full screen unless the video has focus; a show page has no poster and its Playlist, Play next and Add to queue buttons are dark text on dark (contrast 1.4 to 1; 4.5 is needed); the Suggestions page loads blank; and Media requests shows a household member owner-only controls (the server side was not yet proven to refuse them). An automated browser test now exists (10 checks); its first run passed 4 and failed 6, matching the hand findings.

Other results from the same day.

Watch Together failed: with two phones signed in only to the home server, Host and Join looked connected but never joined, because the party service needs a cloud sign-in. Test build 41 now says so honestly and stops retrying. That is an error message, not a working room. The owner decided it should work for anyone with access; a design is written, nothing is built.

Trip Journal failed: “Save as a video” with 2 cards, 2 photos and 1 short clip on a Galaxy S9+ stayed at 0% and gave up after about 30 seconds on each size. The cause is not yet known.

Campsite, no app and no account: a Galaxy S9+ hosted on home Wi-Fi; a Galaxy S24 opened the host’s address in Chrome, typed a name and joined; the host showed “1 guest connected” and the guest page showed the host’s library (644 titles) with Movies, Music and Games. On mobile data only, the same page gave an honest “can’t reach the host” message. Not tested: a guest game round, guest video, a hotspot with a SIM guest.

Photo map: decided as a build (opt-in, your own photos only, map tiles fetched through the home computer only). It is built in a branch with 27 automated tests and a test build exists, but it is not merged, not released and not yet seen on a phone with geotagged photos.

Also on Oct 24: Windows 0.1.102 was published at 1:05 PM Eastern. A second, older 4-core Windows PC updated itself from 0.1.99 to 0.1.102 at 5:41 PM (download matched the feed, signature checked as valid, installed in about a minute), and its log shows Start with Windows launching the app three times between Oct 22 and Oct 24. A review of the new library-share code route found two high-severity limiter and lock problems and several smaller ones; a repair branch is pushed but not cleared, merged or deployed, and the route is off. The bugs we found.

  • Audiobooks: seeking to another chapter while paused can be lost after restarting. Earlier played checkpoints and bookmarks passed. The paused seek still needs a repair and a new installed-build check.
  • Trip Journal: a minimal 720p export still times out on the Galaxy S9+ without producing a video, and on Oct 24 an export with 2 cards, 2 photos and 1 short clip stayed at 0% on every size tried. The cause is not yet known. A recap screen opening is not export success.
  • Watch Together: Start and Join can be clipped at ordinary display density. Earlier sync, chat, reactions and reconnect checks used a temporary density workaround. That does not close the layout bug. On Oct 24, two phones signed in only to the home server could not join a party at all (the party service needs a cloud sign-in); test build 41 now says so honestly. That is an error message, not a working room.
  • Sidecar captions: on the tested Android builds (tester35 and tester36) the subtitle file could be selected, but the expected cue was absent from the picture. A fix was merged into the code line on Oct 24 and is in test build 42, but no phone has shown it yet and it needs to be verified on a phone running the new build. The browser player did draw a separate subtitle file correctly on Oct 24.
  • Warm pairing: opening a new pairing link while the app is already open can leave the old Home visible.
  • Online Next: the player can skip an earlier episode already saved on the phone.

One website bug is resolved: Memory Match now announces the tested hearts and spades pairs correctly, and its complete-game regression passed. The original failure remains in history.

These open issues keep their rows capped. Other older issues, including Campsite mobile-data and an unmatched stop route, stay in the feature record until a matching check closes them.

Chromecast: older failures, newer partial passes in test builds. On Oct 23 the S9+ native route allowed 93 seconds of preparation and a separate silent Live TV attempt waited about 94 seconds; the sender said Casting but four receiver polls showed no media session. Those results describe the older routes tried that day. On Oct 24, on unpublished test builds, the cast controls worked by finger on the real Living Room TV and a streamed film cast with picture and sound; a downloaded copy did not play. The fixes are not in a published download, the open-issue count and the 90% cap are unchanged by this update, and the phone pass-through route, seeking by dragging, TV captions and a second sender are still unproven.

The S24 joined a test Campsite over Wi-Fi with mobile data also on. That is a guest-admission pass. It is not a pass for mobile-data-only reachability. The local guest address was unreachable on cellular alone; that test-route limit does not prove a new product bug.

Code progress is real. It is not a device pass.

Reviewed code merged today includes safer Campsite stop cleanup, casting-ticket handling, phone-speaker placement corrections and several ordinary interface repairs. The Memory Match announcement correction is merged and has now passed its published browser regression. The other changes still need their named final browser, phone, Windows or hardware checks.

The remote-control work has three independent security reviews. It has not made a real end-to-end connection. The original-video safety guard still has review findings to fix. We will not describe either as proven working because code exists.

More work is in the list below. A framework, a review or a passing automated code test does not make it available. Current download versions remain listed on the download page.

In development. Off by default.

Every item here is In development. These are unfinished projects and experiments. They are not offered as available products. None is for sale. A switch or prototype does not establish that it works.

Remote Desktop from anywhere

In development · Off by default. The phone must approve every connection. Three independent security reviews are complete. It has not been connected or tested end to end on real devices.

Phone Help

In development · Off by default. Showing your phone screen to another phone still needs real permission, viewing, disconnect and recovery checks.

Stream from my PC replacement

In development · Off by default. We are replacing the old route. The replacement is not a tested way to watch your PC yet.

3D characters

In development · Off by default. There is a framework and a placeholder rabbit. We are waiting for real models. This is not a finished character feature.

Beebo Play lobby and more browser games

In development · Off by default. The lobby and new connected play need real players, reconnection and completed-game checks. The smaller game tests above do not prove this new work.

Managed hosting

In development · Off by default · Not for sale. Real setup, operation and customer acceptance are still ahead.

Labs

In development · Off by default. A place for clearly marked experiments. An experiment must not look like a finished feature.

A better guest browser player

In development · Off by default. We are working on clearer controls and steadier recovery. Real guest browsers and difficult connections still need acceptance checks.

Steadier phone-speaker sync

In development · Off by default. Control and dispatch checks passed. Tight timing, drift, different phones and sound heard by a person are still required.

Original-video safety guard

In development · Off by default. The goal is an absolute rule: Beebo must never delete or overwrite an original video. The guard still has review findings. That goal is not a guarantee that the unfinished guard already meets it.

Trip and dashcam sync

In development · Off by default. Real trips, camera clocks, interruption and recovery still need testing. A code test is not a road test.

Camera and intercom ideas

In development · Off by default. Early work only. Real devices, clear permissions, sound, stop controls and recovery have not been accepted.

What we still need people or hardware for.

  • A real car and head unit, with a passenger and safe parked checks first.
  • Real Android TV, Fire TV and Roku devices.
  • A NAS, a real Linux PC and a Mac.
  • A real TV tuner and the matching signal.
  • Native speakers to check translations.
  • Screen-reader users and full keyboard journeys.
  • People to hear the sound, check speaker timing and see the real TV picture and captions.

We also still need supported external test routes, normal test accounts and missing fixtures for some automated checks. We will not rename those gaps as human-only work.

We will keep this record dated. A later completed test report can move a row forward. A new code change by itself cannot.
