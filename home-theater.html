Home theater and big screen

Beebo is a media server for your own movies, shows, music and photos. Camping and road trips are one part of it. This page is about the living-room part, and it answers four questions people ask, with a status label on every answer.

The short version. Beebo already converts video with your graphics card, runs on Android TV and Google TV, and plays your original files untouched. Better conversion, Linux and Docker servers, Live TV and more surround-sound options are in Beta for the next release. Apps for Fire TV, Roku, Samsung, LG, Xbox and Apple devices, and passthrough of HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Atmos and DTS:X, are in development, so we do not claim them today.

1. Video conversion, and using your graphics card

When a phone is on mobile data, a TV cannot play a file, or someone picks 1080p, 720p or 480p, your Beebo PC converts the film while it plays. The result is standard H.264 video and AAC sound, sent in small pieces (HLS). Your original files are never touched. Original quality skips the conversion and is never limited.

What the current download does Available now

Windows 0.1.57 tests your PC with a one-second sample encode, because a video tool can list an NVIDIA encoder even when the PC has no NVIDIA card. It then uses the first encoder that really works: NVIDIA (NVENC), Intel Quick Sync or AMD, and otherwise the processor. When it converts an HDR film, it can also tone-map it to standard video using the processor.

The rebuilt engine Beta: next release

Built and covered by our automated tests, but not in Windows 0.1.57.

  • Encoders it can use: NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync and AMD on Windows and Linux, VAAPI on Linux, Apple VideoToolbox on a Mac, and the processor as the always-available last choice. Beebo checks each one with a test encode at start-up and remembers the result. Check again in Settings re-tests after a driver update or a new graphics card.
  • Automatic fallback: if an encoder stops, or shows no picture for 10 seconds while someone waits, the same viewing session moves to the next encoder, picking up right after the last piece it made. It should not end with a dead player, and Beebo writes one plain line to its log for each switch.
  • HDR tone-mapping: several methods, each proven by a test on a sample HDR picture before it is used (VAAPI, Vulkan, OpenCL, or the processor). The picture is scaled down first, so a 4K film played at 720p is cheaper to tone-map. If every method fails, the film still plays, with duller colours.
  • Limits and a waiting line: the owner can set how many conversions run at once (1 to 8), or leave it automatic (1 on a weak PC, 2 normally, 3 on a strong one, plus 1 when a graphics encoder is in use). Someone over the limit is told “The server is busy right now”, given their place in line, and starts by themselves when a slot frees up. First come, first served, with no promised wait time.
  • Old or slow PC: Beebo chooses lighter settings from the core count, speed and memory, and runs conversions at below-normal priority so the rest of the PC stays usable. The owner can override this in Settings.
  • A dashboard tile shows conversions running and waiting, and whether a graphics card is in use. Live TV uses the same encoder chain and fallback.

What this does not do yet In development

  • Conversion always produces H.264 video at up to 1080p. There is no HEVC or AV1 output yet, and Beebo does not yet copy a compatible video stream untouched while converting only the sound.
  • Decoding the source film is still done by the processor, which matters on an old PC with 4K HEVC files. Hardware decoding is not built yet.
  • We have not verified the NVIDIA, AMD, Linux VAAPI or Apple VideoToolbox paths, or the newer HDR tone-map methods, on real hardware. The automated tests use stand-ins. If your graphics card fails, the fallback is designed to carry on with the next encoder.
  • The separate offline “Convert” tool does not use hardware acceleration.

The 4K files you own can still play as the original when your TV or player can handle them. Beebo Relay’s paid tiers are priced by streaming quality; away-from-home viewing with a direct connection, UPnP or your own relay is free. See Watch away from home.

4. Picture and sound formats: what we can and cannot say

We only state what we have verified. Dolby, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos, DTS, DTS:X and HDR10+ are trademarks of their owners, named here only to say what Beebo does and does not do.

Works Available now

  • Original files play untouched. Choose Original quality and Beebo sends the file as it is. What your screen and speakers then show is up to your player and TV.
  • HDR to standard video. When Beebo converts an HDR film, it can tone-map it to standard video so colours do not look washed out, using the processor.
  • Choosing an audio track and subtitles in the player.

Sound options Beta: next release

  • Auto, stereo or surround. Stereo uses a real mix-down with an optional dialogue-focus mix and a limiter.
  • Surround: 5.1 sound as Dolby Digital Plus, Dolby Digital or AAC 5.1, following what the player says it can play. Compatible tracks are copied untouched.
  • Night mode, volume levelling and audio delay.
  • DTS, TrueHD, Opus and FLAC tracks a device cannot play are converted, and Beebo never labels a conversion as Atmos or DTS:X.
  • See Sound and audio. Not yet verified on real receivers and TVs.

HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos and DTS passthrough In development

Sending these formats intact to your TV or receiver is being built. Until it is done and verified on real equipment, we do not claim support for HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Atmos or DTS:X. When Beebo converts a soundtrack, object-based audio such as Atmos is not kept. The next release has an early HDMI passthrough setting in the Android app, but we have not checked it on a real receiver, so we are not counting it as support.

Designed for local-first use; an offline audit is in progress. Your library, watch history and family settings live on your own PC, and streaming at home goes over your own network. What is not there yet

The short, honest list, as of September 21, 2026, for Beebo for Windows 0.1.57 and the Android app 1.38:

  • No Beebo app yet for Fire TV, Roku, Samsung, LG, Xbox, Apple TV, iPhone or iPad. They are in development and untested on real devices.
  • No support claimed for HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos or DTS:X passthrough. It is in development.
  • Conversion is H.264 up to 1080p, with software decoding. No HEVC or AV1 output, no hardware decoding, no video remux yet.
  • The rebuilt transcoding engine, Linux, the Docker server, Live TV and the other Beta items above are not in the current downloads.
  • The NVIDIA, AMD, VAAPI and VideoToolbox conversion paths have not been verified on real hardware, and the Jellyfin-compatible mode has not been tried with real Jellyfin apps.
  • Linux has only been tested by an automated install on Ubuntu 22.04. macOS has never been run on a real Mac.
  • The Windows installer is not code-signed, and there is no published list of tested TV models.
  • The Android app is a test download from this site. A Google Play listing is in closed testing.
  • No PlayStation app, and none planned.
  • Offline behaviour has not been fully audited.

Questions about the big screen

Does Beebo have hardware transcoding?

Yes, in two stages. The current Windows download (0.1.57) already converts video with your PC’s NVIDIA, Intel Quick Sync or AMD encoder when it has one, and falls back to the processor. A rebuilt engine is in Beta for the next release: it tests each encoder, switches to the next one automatically if one fails, tone-maps HDR video, limits how many conversions run at once and adapts to an old PC.

Does Beebo have big-screen apps?

Available now: the Android app on Android TV and Google TV (you install it yourself), casting to Chromecast and Google TV, and AirPlay from Safari. In development, not published and not tested on real devices: Fire TV, Roku, Samsung, LG, Xbox and Apple TV, iPhone and iPad apps.

Does Beebo support HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Atmos and DTS:X?

Not yet. Passthrough of HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos and DTS:X is in development, so Beebo does not claim support. What works today: playing the original file untouched, and converting HDR video to standard dynamic range when a lower quality is chosen. Surround sound options are in Beta for the next release.

Does the Beebo server have to run on Windows?

Today, yes: the released server runs on Windows 10 and 11. A Linux desktop version and a headless server for Docker and NAS boxes are in Beta for the next release, and macOS is in development.

Is Beebo only a travel companion?

No. Camping and road-trip features are one part of Beebo. It is a media server for your own movies, shows, music and photos that plays at home on your phone, browser and TV, with family controls, and works away from home too.

Does Beebo work when the internet is down?

Beebo is designed for local-first use, and an offline audit is in progress. We do not yet claim that every feature has been verified offline. Away-from-home viewing, poster and subtitle lookups and update checks need the internet.
