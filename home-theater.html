The details behind your playback

Your big screen.
The full picture.

Understand what Beebo preserves, what it converts, and what your display and speakers must support. Resolution, HDR and surround sound each have their own requirements.

A better match for the screen and sound system

Keep the original when possible

Compatible original playback avoids re-encoding the picture. Original 4K and HDR depend on the player, cable or casting route, display and connection.

Copy what already works

Remuxing can retain compatible H.264 or HEVC video while changing the container. A soundtrack may need conversion even when the picture remains untouched.

Convert for compatibility

When required, the computer makes a smaller or more compatible stream. Current live output is 480p, 720p or 1080p; optional AV1 output requires opt-in and compatible decoding.

$ Included for local playback
The conversion controls described here do not require a separate Beebo transcoding upgrade for use on your own computer at home. Hardware, electricity and your connection remain yours. Optional Beebo-managed connectivity is a separate service: check the current service offer and limits. A paid connection cannot make an incompatible player or overloaded computer support a format.

Why an HDR movie can look dull after conversion

Converting HDR requires appropriate tone mapping to a standard-range display or stream. Available methods include VAAPI, libplacebo/Vulkan, OpenCL and zscale-based processing, depending on the build and encoder. The minimal macOS build recipe has different filters, so identical tone-mapping support cannot be assumed across operating systems.

If suitable tone-mapping routes fail, a fallback can produce a dull or otherwise incorrect picture. Choose a compatible original route where appropriate, and report the source, selected path and observed output. Do not treat metadata recognition alone as a successful HDR viewing test.

Smooth playback starts with the entire connection

A home-theater stream depends on more than headline resolution. Original video can have much higher and more variable bitrate than a converted profile. Wi-Fi stability, storage read speed, the source computer’s upload speed away from home, receiver support and simultaneous viewers all matter.

Understand the three main limits
  • Delivery: original bitrate plus audio and overhead must fit the path. Peak scenes can exceed the average.
  • Processing: the computer must decode, scale, tone-map, overlay subtitles and encode quickly enough when those operations are requested.
  • Receiving: the player must decode and display the selected format. Server hardware cannot add a codec or HDR mode that the device lacks.

Conversion concurrency is a protective setting, not a promise of a fixed number of 4K streams on every machine. Start with one representative file, then add viewers while watching measured load.

Built features, honest testing labels

A ✓ Built · local checks passed label identifies the stated local verification scope, with real-user/device testing still needed. A ✓ Device tested label requires a recorded test on the stated equipment. Built · verification pending means implementation was inspected without a matching completed test record in this review. Hardware requirements are conditions, not a failure status.

Documented local integration checks cover parts of the direct/remux/HLS pipeline. Receiver modes, dynamic HDR presentation, real television playback, large 4K startup and each hardware/driver combination still need their own evidence. Their presence in the source is described as built; no unperformed equipment test is represented as complete.

A home-theater tester checklist
  1. Record the exact release, device, operating system, display and receiver.
  2. Try an authorized 1080p original and a 4K original. Confirm the selected playback route.
  3. Enable text subtitles, then test a source with image subtitles and observe whether conversion begins.
  4. Select stereo and surround tracks. Check the receiver display for the actual codec/channel mode.
  5. Test HDR original, then a lower-quality conversion; compare color and brightness.
  6. Seek forward/back, pause/resume, and test reconnect without losing audio/subtitle choices.
