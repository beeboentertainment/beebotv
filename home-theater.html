Home theater and big screen

Beebo is a media server for your own movies, shows, music and photos. Camping and road trips are one part of it. This page is about the living-room part, and it answers four questions people ask, with a status label on every answer.

Testers wanted — posted Sep 21, 2026, 4:46 PM Eastern. We need people with real TVs, receivers and soundbars. Real feedback earns a lifetime streaming pass. Help test home theater →

The short version. Beebo for Windows 0.1.58 converts video with your graphics card when it has one, labels your files with 4K, HDR and surround badges, and adds Linux downloads and a Docker image, all in Beta. Android TV and Google TV run the Android app, and original files play untouched. Apps for Fire TV, Roku, Samsung, LG, Xbox and Apple devices are in development. Beebo does not claim HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Atmos or DTS:X passthrough to a receiver, because we have not checked it on real equipment.

1. Video conversion, and using your graphics card

When a phone is on mobile data, a TV cannot play a file, or someone picks 1080p, 720p or 480p, your Beebo PC converts the film while it plays. The result is standard H.264 video and AAC sound, sent in small pieces (HLS). Your original files are never touched. Original quality skips the conversion and is never limited.

The rebuilt engine in Windows 0.1.58 Available now — Beta (Windows 0.1.58)

  • Encoders it can use: NVIDIA NVENC, Intel Quick Sync and AMD on Windows and Linux, VAAPI on Linux, and the processor as the always-available last choice. Beebo checks each one with a test encode at start-up, because a video tool can list an NVIDIA encoder even when the PC has no NVIDIA card, and it remembers the result. Check again in Settings re-tests after a driver update or a new graphics card.
  • Automatic fallback: if an encoder stops, or shows no picture for 10 seconds while someone waits, the same viewing session moves to the next encoder, picking up right after the last piece it made. It should not end with a dead player, and Beebo writes one plain line to its log for each switch.
  • HDR tone-mapping: several methods, each proven by a test on a sample HDR picture before it is used (VAAPI, Vulkan, OpenCL, or the processor). The picture is scaled down first, so a 4K film played at 720p is cheaper to tone-map. If every method fails, the film still plays, with duller colours.
  • Limits and a waiting line: the owner can set how many conversions run at once (1 to 8), or leave it automatic (1 on a weak PC, 2 normally, 3 on a strong one, plus 1 when a graphics encoder is in use). Someone over the limit is told “The server is busy right now”, given their place in line, and starts by themselves when a slot frees up. First come, first served, with no promised wait time.
  • Old or slow PC: Beebo chooses lighter settings from the core count, speed and memory, and runs conversions at below-normal priority so the rest of the PC stays usable. The owner can override this in Settings.
  • A dashboard tile shows conversions running and waiting, and whether a graphics card is in use. Live TV uses the same encoder chain and fallback.

What we have and have not tested. On real hardware we have exercised only Intel Quick Sync and the processor (software) path. NVIDIA NVENC, AMD, Linux VAAPI and the newer HDR tone-map methods have been tested only against stand-ins in our automated tests, not on real graphics cards. If your graphics card fails, the fallback is designed to carry on with the next encoder.

What this does not do yet In development

  • Conversion always produces H.264 video at up to 1080p. There is no HEVC or AV1 output yet.
  • Decoding the source film is still done by the processor, which matters on an old PC with 4K HEVC files. Hardware decoding is not built yet.
  • The separate offline “Convert” tool does not use hardware acceleration.

The 4K files you own can still play as the original when your TV or player can handle them. Beebo Relay’s paid tiers are priced by streaming quality; away-from-home viewing with a direct connection, UPnP or your own relay is free. See Watch away from home. Away from home, Windows 0.1.58 is also about four to five times faster in our own tests of the tunnel that carries the video; that figure is from our tests on a good connection, not a promise for your setup.

4. Picture and sound formats: what we can and cannot say

We only state what we have verified. Dolby, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos, DTS, DTS:X and HDR10+ are trademarks of their owners, named here only to say what Beebo does and does not do.

Works Available now

  • Original files play untouched. Choose Original quality and Beebo sends the file as it is. What your screen and speakers then show is up to your player and TV.
  • HDR to standard video. When Beebo converts an HDR film, it can tone-map it to standard video so colours do not look washed out.
  • Choosing an audio track and subtitles in the player.

Sound options Available now — Beta (Windows 0.1.58)

  • Auto, stereo or surround. Stereo uses a real mix-down with an optional dialogue-focus mix and a limiter.
  • Surround: 5.1 sound as Dolby Digital Plus, Dolby Digital or AAC 5.1, following what the player says it can play. Compatible tracks are copied untouched.
  • Night mode, volume levelling and audio delay.
  • DTS, TrueHD, Opus and FLAC tracks a device cannot play are converted, and Beebo never labels a conversion as Atmos or DTS:X.
  • See Sound and audio. Not yet verified on real receivers and TVs.

File badges and direct-play decisions Available now — Beta (Windows 0.1.58)

Beebo reads each file and shows badges for what it holds: 4K, HDR10, HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos and DTS:X. It also keeps a profile of what each kind of device can play, and decides for each film whether to play the original, copy the compatible parts, or convert only what a device cannot handle. Every decision comes with a plain reason. We checked this against sample descriptions of files and devices, and with real ffmpeg on test clips. We have not checked it on real files of every format, or on a real TV or receiver.

HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos and DTS passthrough to a receiver In development

Sending these formats intact to your TV or receiver is part of the decisions above, but it is unverified on real equipment. Until it is verified, we do not claim support for HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Atmos or DTS:X. When Beebo converts a soundtrack, object-based audio such as Atmos is not kept. The Android test build 1.40 has an early HDMI passthrough setting (Available now — test build (Beta)), which has not been checked on a real receiver either. The Android app’s device profiles for Android TV and Fire TV devices, which tell the server what each device can play (for better direct play, asking for HDR or surround only when the TV reports it), are Available now — test build (Beta) in Android 1.40. They have not been checked on real TVs.

Works when your internet is down

Watching at home never needs the internet. Your library, accounts, saved posters and details, and the phones and TVs on your own Wi-Fi keep working when your internet is down. Away-from-home viewing, looking up brand-new titles, online subtitle searches, YouTube trailers and update checks do need it, and Beebo says so when it is out. We have an automated test that blocks every public network address, but the owner check with a real router unplugged is still to be done, so we do not claim more than this. See Privacy in plain words for what Beebo’s service handles.

What is not there yet

The short, honest list, as of September 21, 2026, for Beebo for Windows 0.1.58 and the Android app 1.40:

  • No Beebo app yet for Fire TV, Roku, Samsung, LG, Xbox, Apple TV, iPhone or iPad. They are in development and have never been run on real devices.
  • No support claimed for HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Dolby Atmos or DTS:X passthrough. It has not been verified on real equipment.
  • Conversion is H.264 up to 1080p, with software decoding. No HEVC or AV1 output and no hardware decoding.
  • Only Intel Quick Sync and software conversion have been exercised on real hardware. NVIDIA, AMD and Linux VAAPI paths have not.
  • Live TV has never been tested on a real HDHomeRun tuner. Phone speakers for movies and Movie Night have only been tested with simulated phones.
  • The compatible third-party mode has not been tried with real compatible third-party apps.
  • Linux is tested on Ubuntu 22.04 in our automated build only, and has no automatic updates. The Docker image is not tested on any specific NAS model. macOS has never been run on a real Mac.
  • The Windows installer is not code-signed, and there is no published list of tested TV models.
  • The Android app is a test download from this site. A Google Play listing is in closed testing.
  • No PlayStation app, and none planned.
  • The owner check of offline use with the router unplugged has not been done yet.

Questions about the big screen

Does Beebo have hardware transcoding?

Yes, in Beta. Windows 0.1.58 tests your PC’s NVIDIA, Intel Quick Sync and AMD encoders, switches to the next one automatically if one fails, tone-maps HDR video, limits how many conversions run at once and adapts to an old PC. On real hardware we have exercised only Intel Quick Sync and the processor path.

Does Beebo have big-screen apps?

Available now: the Android app on Android TV and Google TV (you install it yourself), casting to Chromecast and Google TV, and AirPlay from Safari. In development, never run on real devices: Fire TV, Roku, Samsung, LG, Xbox and Apple TV, iPhone and iPad apps.

Does Beebo support HDR10+, Dolby Vision, Atmos and DTS:X?

Beebo reads these formats from your files, shows badges and decides per device whether to play the original, copy it or convert it. It does not claim passthrough to a receiver, because that is unverified on real equipment. Original files play untouched, and HDR converts to standard video when a lower quality is picked. Object-based audio such as Atmos is not kept when Beebo converts a soundtrack.

Does the Beebo server have to run on Windows?

No. Windows 0.1.58 comes with a Linux desktop download (an AppImage and a .deb, tested on Ubuntu 22.04 in our automated build only) and a Docker image for Linux servers and NAS boxes. Both are Beta. macOS is in development.

Is Beebo only a travel companion?

No. Camping and road-trip features are one part of Beebo. It is a media server for your own movies, shows, music and photos that plays at home on your phone, browser and TV, with family controls, and works away from home too.

Does Beebo work when the internet is down?

Watching at home never needs the internet. Your library, accounts, saved posters and the phones and TVs on your own Wi-Fi keep working. Away-from-home viewing, looking up brand-new titles, online subtitle searches, YouTube trailers and update checks need the internet. The owner check with a real router unplugged is still to be done.
